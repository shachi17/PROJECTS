Project Overview
Building My First RC Aircraft for Avishkar, MNNIT Allahabad

Joining the FALCON 2025 aeromodelling competition at MNNIT was a steep learning curve. Coming from a Biotechnology background, concepts like control surfaces, propulsion, and electronics were entirely new, but I jumped at the opportunity when a friend from Mechanical Engineering invited me to join his team.

Our workshop became an intense, real-time classroom. Over a week, I moved from complete novice to actively handling the aircraft’s physical assembly, electronics integration, and final testing. The journey wasn't smooth—we battled time constraints, inconsistent availability, and last-minute technical setbacks—but the pressure only deepened the experience.

Building that RC aircraft taught me that engineering is less about a single moment of success and more about collaboration, persistence, and meticulous attention to detail. From the first opening of the kit to our final flight, this project transformed how I approach technical challenges and reinforced the value of learning outside my core field.

"Sometimes the most meaningful projects aren't the ones that end with success — they're the ones that change how you think."
Understanding the Kit
Every component, before a single cut was made

At the beginning of the workshop, each team received a complete RC kit containing all the essential materials required to construct a fixed-wing aircraft. The organizers introduced every component and explained its function before assembly began — helping beginners like us understand how the aircraft's mechanical structure and electronics would eventually work together.

✈ Airframe Materials

  • Foam-board sheets for fuselage, wings & tail
  • Pre-designed templates for cutting & folding
  • Adhesive material

⚡ Propulsion System

  • Motor
  • Propeller
  • Electronic Speed Controller (ESC)

Generated the thrust required for flight.

🎮 Flight Control System

  • Servo Motors
  • Receiver
  • Push Rods

Converted pilot commands into precise control-surface movement.

🔋 Power System

  • Battery
  • Connectors
  • Power distribution wiring

The aircraft's primary energy source.

Before building anything, we carefully identified each component, matched it with the assembly guide, and familiarized ourselves with the construction sequence demonstrated by the instructors.

Assembly Process

Once we identified every component, construction began. We strictly followed the provided tutorial to ensure structural accuracy, using lightweight foam-board as our primary material. Mastering the various cutting techniques—including complete cuts, partial paper-layer removal, and precise 45° bevel cuts for tail surfaces—was essential for shaping the airframe.

We used hot glue for most joints, which demanded quick, precise positioning before the adhesive hardened. Despite some initial confusion, the structure steadily took shape. Once the airframe was complete, we installed and tested the motor, servos, receiver, and control rods.

I was involved in nearly every stage of the process, from joining structural components to fine-tuning the control surfaces. These hands-on tasks, from initial assembly to verifying the mechanical systems, gave me a comprehensive understanding of the aircraft's internal and external construction.

Mid Evaluation

During the competition, each team underwent a mid-evaluation viva to track progress. Despite joining the project with no prior experience, I dedicated time to mastering the aircraft’s components, electronics, and flight principles to address the evaluators' questions.

The viva was a nerve-wracking experience, but it forced me to think on my feet and effectively articulate complex technical concepts. Successfully representing the team under pressure not only validated my hard work but also significantly boosted my confidence in my ability to learn and explain unfamiliar subject matter.

Adding the Finishing Touches

With the structural assembly complete, we focused on the aircraft's appearance. The aircraft was painted using a camouflage-inspired design, giving the plain foam-board aircraft a more realistic and polished look. Beyond aesthetics, this stage reflected the effort and attention to detail that went into the project, making the aircraft feel truly complete before its first flight.

Chapter 3 — Competition Day

The Final Preparations

After a week of intense work, we finished our aircraft by installing the landing gear and performing final system checks. Arriving at the competition ground, the energy was electric; seeing teams from across the college making final adjustments to their planes made every hour spent in the workshop feel worth it.

The Flight

When our turn finally came, the takeoff was smooth, providing a brief, rewarding moment of success. Watching our creation—built from scratch over countless hours—actually take flight was an unforgettable experience.

Unfortunately, the flight was short-lived. Shortly after takeoff, the propeller detached from the motor, causing a loss of thrust that led to a crash before we could complete the round.

Looking Back

During the workshop, a senior had emphasized the critical importance of securely tightening the propeller with a specialized tool before every flight. I was doubtful about this concern after we reattached the propeller during final assembly

In hindsight, that single detail proved fatal. The aircraft crashed in the first round, sustaining significant damage. Watching it break apart was heartbreaking; it felt like a week of intense learning and collective effort had been undone in a matter of seconds.

Picking Up the Pieces

The crash was devastating. Watching our week of hard work destroyed in seconds was difficult to process, and I initially walked away from the venue, convinced our journey was over.

The following day, however, my teammates repaired the aircraft and got it flying again. Seeing it soar was a bittersweet reminder that engineering is rarely about perfection on the first try; it is fundamentally about learning from failure, rebuilding, and persisting despite setbacks.

Flying Alongside an Eagle

As I watched the aircraft soar through the sky, with an eagle briefly flying beside it, I couldn't help but smile. It felt like the perfect ending to a journey that had begun with nothing more than sheets of foam, a box of electronics, and a group of students willing to learn.

Final Reflection

By the competition's end, I had navigated the entire lifecycle of an engineering project: from assembly and technical testing to the highs of flight and the lows of failure. What began as a venture into unfamiliar territory evolved into one of my most memorable experiences.

Beyond the technical challenges, I was inspired by the senior participants. Their ability to handle aircraft with such precision and confidence revealed the true nature of mastery—it isn't about luck, but the culmination of endless practice and dedication.

FALCON taught me that engineering rests on precision, communication, and trust. While a single missed detail can alter an outcome, setbacks are not the end of the story—they are opportunities to learn and improve. I left without a medal, but gained something far more valuable: the confidence to step outside my discipline, contribute meaningfully, and embrace the continuous process of learning.

Sometimes, the best projects aren't the ones that end in victory.
They're the ones that leave you looking at the sky a little differently.
