Project Overview

FALCON 2025 was an aeromodelling competition organized as part of Avishkar, the annual technical festival of Motilal Nehru National Institute of Technology (MNNIT), Prayagraj. The challenge required participating teams to assemble an RC aircraft from a kit provided by the organizers and successfully fly it during the competition. Teams were also expected to practlce flying the aircraft using simulation software before attempting the real flight.

Coming from a Biotechnology background, I had never worked on an aircraft before. Every concept—from control surfaces and propulsion systems to servos and electronic speed controllers—was completely new to me. Yet, when a friend from the Mechanical Engineering department invited me to join the team, saw an opportunity to learn something entirely outside my field.

Over the following week, our workshop became more than just a place to build an airplane. It became a classroom Where I learned by observing, experimenting, making mistakes, and solving problems in real time. contributed extensively to the aircraft's physical assembly, electronics integration, component testing, and final adjustments while learning the fundamentals of RC aircraft construction from scratch.

Like many engineering projects, the journey wasn't straightforward. We faced time constraints, inconsistent team availability, last-minute assembly challenges, technical setbacks.

The experience left me with something far more valuable than a medal—it taught me how engineering projects are built through collaboration, persistence, continuous learning, and attention to the smallest details. I have documented that journey—from opening the aircraft kit for the very first time to watching our completed aircraft take flight, experiencing failure, and reflecting on the lessons that continue to influence how I approach projects today,

"Sometimes the most meaningful projects aren't the ones that end with success — they're the ones that change how you think."
Understanding the Kit
Every component, before a single cut was made

At the beginning of the workshop, each team received a complete RC kit containing all the essential materials required to construct a fixed-wing aircraft. The organizers introduced every component and explained its function before assembly began — helping beginners like us understand how the aircraft's mechanical structure and electronics would eventually work together.

Before building anything, we carefully identified each component, matched it with the assembly guide, and familiarized ourselves with the construction sequence demonstrated by the instructors.

Assembly Process

With the kit understood and every component identified, construction finally began. Rather than improvising the design, we closely followed the prescribed construction sequence to ensure structural accuracy from the tutorial provided.

The primary building material was a lightweight foam-board composed of two paper layers surrounding a foam core. Different sections of the aircraft required different cutting techniques depending on the type of fold being created — complete cuts, partial cuts that removed only the paper layer, foam-only cuts, and precise 45° bevel cuts for components such as the tail surfaces.

Most joints were assembled using hot glue, which provided quick setting times while maintaining adequate structural strength. Because the adhesive hardened rapidly, every component had to be positioned correctly before the glue cooled.

Although there was confusion at the start, we slowly saw the entire structure of the craft come together. Once the basic airframe was complete, electronic components such as the motor, servos, receiver, and control rods were installed and tested. I gradually became involved in nearly every stage of physical assembly — from joining structural components and installing electronics to adjusting control surfaces and testing the aircraft's mechanical systems before its first flight.

Mid Evaluation

As part of the competition, each team had to present their progress during a mid-evaluation viva. Since some of my teammates were unavailable, I took the responsibility of representing our team. Having joined the project with no prior knowledge of RC aircraft, I spent time understanding the aircraft's components, electronics, and basic flight principles so I could confidently answer the evaluators' questions. Although it was a nerve-wracking experience, the viva challenged me to think on my feet and strengthened my confidence in learning and explaining unfamiliar technical concepts under pressure.

The Reality Behind the Build

One challenge that no one talks about in engineering competitions is everything that happens outside the workshop. Our aircraft wasn't something we could simply fold up and carry in a backpack. Transporting the completed airframe between the hostel and the workshop became a task in itself — every trip required careful handling to avoid damaging delicate components like the wings, landing gear, or tail surfaces. More than once, carrying the aircraft across campus felt harder than building it.

Adding to the pressure, our team's participation was finalized quite late, leaving us with very little time to complete the aircraft. Almost every task happened against the clock, making careful planning even more important.

Looking back, those long hours in the workshop taught me far more than just aircraft construction. They taught me patience, adaptability, and the importance of staying calm even when deadlines seem impossible.

Chapter 3 — Competition Day

The Final Preparations

After nearly a week of continuous work, our aircraft was finally ready for its first official flight. The only task left from the previous day was attaching the landing gear, after which we carried out the final checks and prepared for competition. The atmosphere on the competition ground was filled with excitement — teams from different departments gathered with their aircraft, making last-minute adjustments and waiting for their turn. Seeing so many completed RC planes lined up on the field made all the hours spent in the workshop feel worthwhile.

The Flight

When our turn arrived, the aircraft took off smoothly, and for a brief moment it felt like all our hard work had paid off. Watching something we had built from foam sheets, electronics, and countless hours of effort leave the ground was an unforgettable experience.

Unfortunately, the flight lasted only a few moments. Shortly after takeoff, the propeller detached from the motor, causing the aircraft to lose thrust and crash before completing the round.

Looking Back

During the workshop, one of the seniors had repeatedly emphasized that the propeller must be tightened securely using a special tool before every flight. I remembered this instruction and asked multiple times whether it had been tightened correctly after the propeller was reattached during final assembly. I was assured that everything had been taken care of.

Looking back, that small detail proved to be critical. The aircraft was eliminated in the very first round, and the crash caused significant damage. Watching the aircraft break apart was heartbreaking — beyond the damaged plane, it represented a week of learning, teamwork, and effort that had come to an end within seconds.

Picking Up the Pieces

The crash left me completely devastated. Watching the aircraft we had spent an entire week building get destroyed within seconds was difficult to process. Feeling disheartened, I quietly walked away from the competition venue, convinced that our journey had ended there.

The following day, however, my teammates repaired the damaged aircraft and managed to get it back into flying condition. Seeing the aircraft soar again was a bittersweet moment — it reminded me that engineering is rarely about getting everything right on the first attempt. More often, it's about learning from failure, rebuilding, and trying again.

Flying Alongside an Eagle

As I watched the aircraft soar through the sky, with an eagle briefly flying beside it, I couldn't help but smile. It felt like the perfect ending to a journey that had begun with nothing more than sheets of foam, a box of electronics, and a group of students willing to learn.

Final Reflection

Coming into FALCON 2025 as a Biotechnology student, I knew almost nothing about RC aircraft. By the end of the competition, I had assembled an aircraft, worked with motors and electronics, adjusted control surfaces, presented a technical viva, and experienced both the excitement of flight and the disappointment of failure. Somewhere along the way, an unfamiliar project became one of the most memorable engineering experiences I've ever had.

What impressed me most wasn't just the aircraft itself — it was the people behind it. Watching senior participants fly machines with such confidence and precision was nothing short of inspiring. Every smooth takeoff, controlled turn, and perfect landing reflected countless hours of practice, patience, and dedication. They made unraveling incredibly complex look effortless, and that is what true mastery often looks like.

FALCON also taught me a lesson I'll carry into every future project: engineering is built on precision, communication, and trust. A single overlooked detail can change an outcome, but setbacks don't define a project — the willingness to learn, rebuild, and improve does.

I may not have left with a medal, but I left with something more valuable: the confidence to step into unfamiliar territory, contribute meaningfully, and keep learning beyond the boundaries of my own discipline.
